Study Guide: Late Bleeding in Pregnancy (APH)

Part 1: Basics

Objectives (both decks)
  • Define late bleeding / APH and identify its major causes.
  • Differentiate placenta praevia from placental abruption using key clinical findings (النتائج الإكلينيكية).
  • Describe signs and symptoms; recognize maternal and fetal danger signs.
  • Explain risk factors and complications.
  • Describe initial assessment, nursing priorities, management principles and patient education.
  • Give nursing care that promotes maternal and fetal well-being.
Definition

Late bleeding = Antepartum Hemorrhage (APH) (نزيف قبل الولادة) = bleeding from or into the genital tract (الجهاز التناسلي) during the second half of pregnancy and before birth. “Into” means hidden bleeding inside the uterus.

  • PPT: RCOG (الكلية الملكية البريطانية لأطباء النساء) describes APH from 24+0 weeks (= 24 weeks and 0 days) until birth.
  • Photo deck: after 20 weeks of gestation (عمر الحمل) and before birth.
  • It is an obstetric emergency (حالة طوارئ توليد) because blood loss may threaten both mother and fetus (الجنين).
⚠️ Two definitions: PPT = from 24+0 weeks (RCOG); photo deck = after 20 weeks. Learn both and name the source in the exam. In the early-pregnancy guide, bleeding before 20 weeks is “early bleeding”, so do not mix them. In the photo table, “placenta praevia” listed as a risk factor of praevia probably means previous praevia. The photo deck covers slides 1-12 only.
The main causes (slide 4 / photo 3-4)
  1. Placenta praevia (مشيمة سابقة / قدام عنق الرحم): placenta implanted in the lower uterine segment, partly or completely covering the internal os.
  2. Placental abruption (انفصال المشيمة): premature separation of a normally implanted placenta from the uterine wall.
  3. Vasa praevia (أوعية الجنين قدام العنق): unprotected fetal blood vessels in the membranes over or near the internal os.
  4. Uterine rupture (تمزق الرحم): uncommon but life-threatening.
  5. Local genital tract causes: cervical lesions (آفات / مشاكل بعنق الرحم), infection, trauma (إصابة), malignancy (ورم خبيث).
  6. Placenta accreta spectrum (التصاق المشيمة بعمق في جدار الرحم) may be associated with major obstetric hemorrhage (نزيف توليدي شديد).
💡 Think of the placenta (المشيمة) as the source: praevia = placenta in the wrong place (low), abruption = placenta comes off the wall, vasa praevia = the baby’s vessels are in the wrong place. ➕ First question to ask: is the bleeding painful or painless? It points to the cause.
➕ Why it is dangerous: the uterus and placenta hold a lot of blood, and the baby gets oxygen only through the placenta. A pregnant woman’s blood volume is increased, so she may look stable and then collapse suddenly. A rising pulse (النبض) is the early warning, before blood pressure falls.

Part 2: Placenta Praevia (PPT 5-6 / Photo 5, 9)

What it is

The placenta lies in the lower uterine segment (الجزء السفلي من الرحم) and reaches or covers the internal cervical os (الفتحة الداخلية لعنق الرحم). It is commonly identified by ultrasound (سونار).

TypeWhere the placenta is
Complete (كامل)Completely covers the os
Partial (جزئي)Partially covers the os
Marginal (حافي)Reaches the edge of the os
Low-lying (واطية)Close to the os but does not reach it (edge near, not covering)
Typical presentation
  • Classically sudden, painless, bright-red (أحمر فاتح) vaginal bleeding in the second half of pregnancy.
  • Bleeding may be recurrent (بيتكرر) and can become heavy.
  • No uterine tenderness (ألم عند اللمس); uterus soft and relaxed. Bleeding increases with exertion (مجهود) or intercourse.
  • Malpresentation (وضع غير طبيعي للجنين) may occur because the placenta occupies the lower uterine segment.
  • Maternal vital signs (العلامات الحيوية) and fetal condition must be assessed promptly. Fetal condition is usually normal early.
Risk factors

Previous cesarean birth (ولادة قيصرية), assisted reproductive technology (الحقن المجهري وعلاج الإنجاب), other uterine procedures; (photo deck) multiparity (ولادات كتير سابقة), smoking, previous praevia.

💡 The placenta sits over the cervix. When the lower segment stretches late in pregnancy, the placental vessels tear and fresh blood escapes freely, so it is bright red. The uterus is not squeezed by trapped blood, so it stays soft and painless. Exertion or intercourse disturbs that area, so bleeding increases.
⚠️ Never do a digital vaginal exam (فحص مهبلي بالإصبع) when praevia is suspected: a finger can disturb the low placenta and cause massive bleeding. Ultrasound first.

Part 3: Placental Abruption (PPT 7-8 / Photo 6, 9)

What it is

Premature partial or complete separation (انفصال مبكر جزئي أو كلي) of a normally implanted placenta from the uterine wall before delivery. Blood collects behind it as a retroplacental hematoma (تجمع دم ورا المشيمة).

TypeMeaning
Revealed (ظاهر)Blood escapes through the vagina
Concealed (مخفي)Blood collects behind the placenta
Mixed (مختلط)Both
Typical presentation
  • Sudden severe abdominal or back pain; abdominal/back pain is common.
  • Vaginal bleeding may be dark red (أحمر غامق), but the amount seen outside may underestimate (يقلل من الحقيقي) the real blood loss (it may be concealed).
  • Uterine tenderness and rigidity (تيبّس): uterus is tender, board-like (متيبس زي اللوح), with increased tone (شد عضلة الرحم).
  • Fetal heart rate (نبض الجنين) abnormalities with significant abruption; fetal distress (ضيق الجنين ونقص أكسجينه) or death.
  • Severe cases: coagulopathy (اضطراب تجلط الدم). It can cause maternal shock (صدمة) and fetal compromise.
Risk factors

Hypertension (ضغط الدم المرتفع), trauma, smoking, previous abruption, older age, cocaine use (مخدر الكوكايين).

💡 Blood collects behind the placenta and cannot all escape, so the visible bleeding underestimates the loss. The trapped blood pushes into the muscle, making the uterus hard and painful. The detached part cannot feed the baby, so fetal distress follows. ➕ Diagnosis is mainly clinical (من الأعراض والفحص): ultrasound may help but a normal scan does not exclude abruption.

Part 4: Vasa Praevia (PPT 10 / Photo 7)

What it is

Unprotected fetal blood vessels run through the membranes (الأغشية المحيطة بالجنين) near the internal cervical os. Rupture may occur with membrane rupture (نزول المية) or during labor.

  • Bleeding can be small in volume but dangerous because it is fetal blood.
  • Acute fetal heart rate abnormalities may occur.
  • Antenatal diagnosis (التشخيص قبل الولادة) by ultrasound can allow planned management.
💡 These vessels belong to the baby, not the mother, and they have no cord or placenta tissue protecting them. ➕ A baby has only a small total blood volume (a few hundred mL), so even a small loss is dangerous. ➕ Often linked with the cord attaching to the membranes instead of the placenta (velamentous cord (الحبل السري متصل بالأغشية)); delivery is usually planned by cesarean before labor.

➕ Memory trick: Praevia = Painless, Placenta low, Pliable (soft) uterus, bright red. Abruption = Abdominal pain, All-hard uterus (board-like), Abnormal fetal heart. But the slides stress that presentation helps differentiate and does not replace investigation.

Part 6: Risk Factors (Photo 10 + PPT 5, 7)

Photo deck list
  • Previous cesarean section or uterine surgery
  • Multiparity (ولادات كتير سابقة); advanced maternal age (سن الأم المتقدم)
  • Smoking; hypertensive disorders (اضطرابات ضغط الدم)
  • Abdominal trauma; use of cocaine
  • Short interpregnancy interval (فترة قصيرة بين حملين)
ConditionMain risk factors
Placenta praeviaPrevious CS, uterine procedures, ART, multiparity, smoking
Placental abruptionHypertension, trauma, smoking, previous abruption, older age, cocaine use
💡 Link the risk factor to the mechanism: surgery and many births scar the uterine lining, so the placenta implants low. High blood pressure, cocaine and trauma cause sudden separation of the placenta.

Part 7: Nursing Assessment and Care (PPT 11-14 / Photo 11-12)

ABC approach (خطوات الطوارئ الأساسية)
  • A, Airway: ensure it is open and prepare emergency support if needed.
  • B, Breathing: give oxygen; assess respiratory rate and SpO2 (نسبة الأكسجين في الدم).
  • C, Circulation: stop the bleeding, obtain IV access (كانيولا في الوريد), give fluids, cross-match (تطابق دم المتبرع) blood, monitor vital signs. Assess pulse, BP, perfusion (وصول الدم للأنسجة), mental status (الحالة الذهنية والوعي) and visible blood loss. Take blood samples per local protocol.
  • Assess fetal status promptly after maternal stabilization.
Nursing assessment (photo 11)
  • Assess ABCs; determine gestational age.
  • Assess amount, color and frequency of bleeding.
  • Assess uterine activity and tenderness.
  • Assess fetal heart rate and condition; monitor vital signs.
  • Assess risk factors and history; prepare for emergency management.
Focused history
  • Gestational age and onset of bleeding; amount, color, pattern.
  • Abdominal/back pain or contractions (انقباضات); fetal movement and any change.
  • History of praevia, previous cesarean, hypertension, trauma, previous abruption.
  • Dizziness, weakness or fainting.
Physical and fetal assessment
  • Repeat vital signs and watch for shock.
  • Assess uterine tenderness, tone and contractions.
  • Assess fetal heart rate with appropriate monitoring (متابعة).
  • Ultrasound as indicated for placental location and fetal condition.
  • Do a vaginal examination only when praevia has been excluded and it is clinically appropriate; avoid digital vaginal examination when praevia is suspected.
MeasureWhat it tells you
Pulse, BP, perfusionEarly shock ➕ (pulse rises first)
Urine output (كمية البول)➕ Kidney blood flow, a sign of how well organs are perfused
Fetal heart rate monitoringBaby’s oxygen status
UltrasoundPlacental location and fetal condition
Blood samples, cross-matchReady for transfusion
Coagulation tests (تحاليل التجلط)➕ Detects clotting problems (DIC), mainly in abruption
Immediate nursing priorities (slide 14)
  1. Call the obstetric team and follow the local major hemorrhage / emergency protocol (بروتوكول الطوارئ).
  2. Position safely, keep her warm, give reassurance (طمأنة).
  3. Monitor vital signs, mental status, urine output and ongoing blood loss.
  4. Prepare IV access, blood tests, cross-match, fluids / blood products as prescribed.
  5. Continuous or appropriate fetal monitoring according to gestation and clinical condition.
  6. Document assessment, interventions, response and communication.
💡 Stabilize the mother first: the baby depends on her circulation and oxygen. If she is stable, the baby has the best chance.

Part 8: Medical Management (PPT 15-16)

Placenta praevia
  • Depends on gestational age, bleeding severity, maternal condition, fetal status and placental location. Ultrasound defines the location.
  • Expectant management (متابعة وانتظار من غير تدخل فوري) may be considered in selected stable pregnancies with specialist follow-up.
  • Major or recurrent bleeding may need hospital admission and planned or emergency delivery.
  • Cesarean birth may be required when the placenta obstructs (يسد) the birth canal (قناة الولادة).
Placental abruption
  • Stabilize the mother first while assessing the fetus.
  • Monitor for maternal shock, ongoing blood loss and coagulopathy.
  • Continuous fetal assessment is important when the fetus is viable (قادر على الحياة).
  • Delivery is decided by maternal status, fetal condition, gestational age and severity. Prepare for urgent birth if mother or baby is compromised.
➕ Extra points often added in practice: Rh-negative mothers (فصيلة دم سالبة) may need anti-D injection (حقنة anti-D); if preterm birth is likely, doctors may give antenatal corticosteroids (ستيرويد لنضج رئة الجنين) to mature the baby’s lungs. Follow local protocol.

Part 9: Complications and Patient Education (PPT 17-19)

Maternal complications
  • Hypovolemic shock (صدمة نقص حجم الدم) and anemia (أنيميا).
  • Coagulopathy / DIC (اضطراب تجلط الدم المنتشر), particularly with severe abruption.
  • Need for blood transfusion (نقل دم) and emergency surgery.
  • Acute kidney injury (فشل كلوي حاد) and other consequences of severe blood loss.
  • Major obstetric hemorrhage can be life-threatening.
Fetal and neonatal complications
  • Fetal hypoxia (نقص أكسجين الجنين) or acute fetal compromise.
  • Preterm birth (ولادة مبكرة قبل 37 أسبوع).
  • Fetal growth restriction (قصور نمو الجنين) may be associated with placental disease.
  • Stillbirth (ولادة جنين ميت) can occur in severe cases.
  • Neonatal complications from prematurity or hypoxia.
💡 Heavy bleeding lowers oxygen delivery to mother and baby. A detached placenta releases substances that upset clotting, which leads to DIC.
Patient education
  • Any vaginal bleeding in later pregnancy needs prompt assessment; do not delay contacting the maternity unit because bleeding seems small.
  • Report immediately: abdominal pain, contractions, dizziness, fainting, reduced fetal movement, increasing bleeding.
  • Known low-lying placenta: follow the individualized plan (خطة خاصة بيها) from the obstetric team.
  • Keep antenatal appointments (مواعيد متابعة الحمل) and scheduled ultrasound follow-up.

Part 10: Case Studies and Key Points (PPT 20-24)

Case 1: 34 weeks, sudden painless bright-red bleeding, no pain, stable vitals
  1. Suspect: placenta praevia.
  2. Immediate assessment: ABC, vital signs, amount of bleeding, history, uterine tone and tenderness; IV access and bloods.
  3. Avoid: digital vaginal examination until praevia is excluded.
  4. Fetal assessment: fetal heart rate monitoring; ultrasound for placental location and fetal condition.
Case 2: 36 weeks, abdominal pain, uterine tenderness, bleeding, abnormal fetal heart rate
  1. Suspected: placental abruption.
  2. Maternal complications to monitor: hypovolemic shock, anemia, coagulopathy/DIC, acute kidney injury, need for transfusion or surgery.
  3. Nursing priorities: stabilize the mother, call the team, IV access / bloods / cross-match, monitor vitals and urine output, continuous fetal monitoring, prepare for urgent birth.
  4. Why visible loss underestimates: blood can be concealed behind the placenta.
Key points and summary
  • Treat late bleeding as potentially serious until assessed; stabilize the mother while quickly evaluating the fetus.
  • Painful bleeding suggests abruption; painless bleeding classically suggests praevia.
  • No digital vaginal exam if praevia is suspected. Monitor for shock even when visible bleeding is limited.
  • Presentation helps tell causes apart but does not replace investigation. Ultrasound is essential for placental localization.
  • Early recognition and rapid multidisciplinary management (فريق من تخصصات مختلفة) improve safety. Nurses lead assessment, monitoring, emergency preparation, education and documentation.

References: RCOG Green-top Guideline 63 (Antepartum Haemorrhage); 27a (Placenta Praevia and Accreta Spectrum, updated 30 June 2026); 27b (Vasa Praevia). Follow local hospital protocols.
